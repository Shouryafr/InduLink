How it works

Rules you can read, on data you can check

InduLink is decision support. It structures a challenge, checks hard constraints, scores the rest transparently, and hands the decision to a person with the reasoning attached.

The chain

  1. A challenge is submitted. An industry partner writes a brief with skills, required levels, weights, team size, dates and visibility.
  2. Requirements are structured and approved. Weights are normalised to 100% and shown as percentages. Editing an approved challenge creates a new requirement version; the old one stays readable.
  3. Evidence is evaluated. A student's declared level and their assessed level are separate fields. Only reviewed or verified evidence confirms a level.
  4. Candidates and teams are recommended. Hard checks produce Eligible, Provisionally eligible or Not eligible. Teams are then scored as a whole for coverage and balance, not by adding up individual ranks.
  5. A mentor is recommended. Expertise, domain, experience and remaining capacity are filtered and ranked, and capacity is re-read inside the confirming transaction.
  6. Execution is tracked, and outcomes recorded. Deliverables are reviewed against a rubric, and only then does a competency reach a student's passport — with its scope stated.

The evidence policy, in full

One policy, applied everywhere. It is the reason a match shows two numbers instead of one.

How a match is scored

Within each eligibility group, the match is the weighted sum of each skill's contribution, where a skill contributes the lower of the student's effective level and the required level: 

match = 100 × Σ ( wᵢ × min( Lᵢ / Rᵢ , 1 ) )      with  Σ wᵢ = 1

Lᵢ is the student's effective level for skill i under the evidence policy, Rᵢ the required level, and wᵢ the normalised weight. A skill with no data contributes nothing and is listed as no data rather than scored as a failure.

What InduLink does not do

  • It does not predict a student's success, ability or employability.
  • It does not use machine learning, and makes no machine-learning claims.
  • It does not infer character, reliability or conflict from personal traits.
  • It does not replace a confirmed assignment without a person confirming the change.
  • It does not publish anything on a passport that a student has not chosen to share.

Privacy in one paragraph

Profiles are private by default. A student chooses what industry partners and mentors can see. Evidence review notes are visible only to the student, the reviewer and administrators. Access to confidential project material requires an acknowledgement, and every confidential access is audited.
