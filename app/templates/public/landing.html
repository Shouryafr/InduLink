The connected workflow

One chain, from brief to verified competency

A challenge is submitted and its requirements approved first. Everything after that runs on evidence rather than claims.

  1. Evidence Evidence is submitted, then reviewed against a written level rubric.
  2. Match Hard constraints are checked first; soft scores explain the rest.
  3. Team Teams are scored as a whole — complementary coverage, not star individuals.
  4. Mentor Mentor fit is ranked on expertise, domain, capacity and availability.
  5. Execution Tasks, milestones and deliverables are tracked with versioned requirements.
  6. Verified competencies Outcomes are reviewed and recorded on a scoped skill passport.

Honest by design

How recommendations work

Rules and arithmetic, not a model. Nothing here predicts whether a student will succeed, and nothing here replaces a human decision.

  1. Hard constraints come first Mandatory skills, eligibility, availability and capacity are pass/fail checks that run before any score. A high score never overrides a failed mandatory requirement.
  2. Unknown is not zero A skill with no evidence is shown as no data, not as a failure. Every match shows a confirmed score and a provisional score side by side.
  3. Six separate fields Declared level, assessed level, evidence status, match score, readiness and mentor fit are stored and displayed separately. They are never merged into one number.
  4. Every number is explainable Each recommendation shows its components, weights, gaps, data limitations and when the underlying record was last updated.
  5. People confirm, software proposes Confirmations are explicit, reversible and audited. No confirmed assignment is ever replaced silently, and every correction needs a written reason.
  6. No-fit is a real answer When nothing satisfies the constraints, InduLink says so and lists clearly-labelled near matches. It never fabricates a recommendation to fill the space.

Synthetic demo data

Every record in this environment is synthetic: fictional students, mentors, companies and projects. Synthetic data can demonstrate behaviour. It cannot establish real-world effectiveness, and nothing in this prototype should be read as a claim that it does.
